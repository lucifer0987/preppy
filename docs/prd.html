Preppy

A closed-cohort daily mock-test platform for IBPS Specialist Officer (IT) aspirants. A paper unlocks in a window set when it is scheduled, and a day may hold more than one, scored on the real marking scheme, ranked on a leaderboard that never resets.

Phases 1 and 2 are built. This document now records what exists and why, including the decisions that changed during the build. For how it is put together and how to run it, see the engineering reference.

Every one of these cuts removes a thing that could break, go stale, or need invalidating. The read-time leaderboard in particular means a rescore has no cache to fix.

01Overview

The problem

Serious IBPS SO aspirants practise alone. Commercial test series are generic — built for the broad banking pool, not CSE professional knowledge — expensive, and they give a percentile against an anonymous mass that means nothing. A study group that wants to drill together every night has no tool that combines real exam conditions, real marking, a persistent ranking among people they actually know, and the ability to run their own question bank.

Who it's for

The Aspirant. Final-year CSE student or working IT professional, 21–27. Studies at night, owns a laptop, and is motivated far more by beating four people they know than by a percentile against 200,000 strangers.

The Admin. One person sources the questions, formats them into the paper format, and publishes a paper a day. Not a developer. Their daily cost must stay under five minutes.

Goals

#Goal
G1Reproduce IBPS exam conditions faithfully enough that a Preppy score predicts a real score
G2Make the daily test a habit — a fixed appointment, with streaks and rank as the hook
G3Keep the admin's daily publishing cost under five minutes
G4Make every past test a permanent, browsable study asset
G5Sustain competition across months via a cumulative leaderboard

Explicitly out of scope

Financial Awareness, General Awareness and Computer Awareness · any Hindi variant — English only · disciplines other than CSE · live synchronous multiplayer · public sign-up · payments · native mobile apps · webcam or AI proctoring · AI question generation · forums and doubt-solving · adaptive difficulty · any social feature beyond the leaderboard.

All six fit the six section codes the format allows, which is why the ceiling is six and not four: Professional Knowledge, Reasoning, English and Quantitative Aptitude map straight across, Computer Knowledge onto COMPUTER_AWARENESS, and Financial Awareness onto GENERAL_AWARENESS — carried as a label, since a track names its own sections (FR-6.10.3) and there is no FINANCIAL_AWARENESS code. Nothing here needs a schema change to become a track; it needs somebody to write 240 questions a night, which is the actual reason it is not one.

What both exams do, and Preppy with them

  • Five options, one correct. "Out of the five answers to a question only one will be the correct answer." Preppy runs four instead — a deliberate divergence, with its cost, under FR-7.3.
  • Tests are separately timed. The official tables head their duration column "Time allotted for each test (Separately timed)", and a candidate may attempt any question "within the given duration in each test" — that test's duration, not the paper's. Time not spent in a section is lost, which is why Preppy's timers are per section and never carry over.
  • The penalty is a quarter of what the question is worth, not a flat quarter mark: "one fourth or 0.25 of the marks assigned to that question will be deducted". Blank costs nothing.
  • Marks per question are not uniform. Professional Knowledge carries two marks a question in both the SO preliminary and the SO main, so a wrong answer there costs 0.5 while a wrong answer elsewhere costs 0.25. Preppy stores marks per section, so a pattern can say this; the shipped default does not — see the note under FR-3.3.

Where these numbers come from

Read off the official documents rather than a coaching site, on 28 September 2026: CRP SPL-XVI (the detailed notification dated 30 June 2026, for 2027-28 vacancies) for both SO tables, and CRP RRBs-XIV (advertisement dated 19 September 2025) for the RRB table. The five-option and penalty wording is quoted from an IBPS Information Handout, which is where the per-question mechanics live — the notification does not state them. IBPS reserves the right to change any of this and says so in both documents, so re-check against the current notification before trusting the table above.

03Pattern & scoring

FR-3.1Sections, in a fixed order, each with an independent timer. Which sections, and how many, is the track's pattern (FR-6.10.2); the questions, minutes and marking per section are configurable there or in the paper's own file. Shipped default: four sections, 55 questions, 45 minutes.

Total5545:0049

FR-3.2Section order, question counts and durations are stored per test and overridable in the paper file.

✓ Pacing is correct

48 s/q for Quant, Reasoning and PK is an exact match for the SO preliminary, where all four tests give 25 questions 20 minutes, and an exact match for the Main's Professional Knowledge at 50 questions in 40 minutes. It is slightly tighter than the Main's other three tests, which run 50 to 52.5 s/q.

English at 54 s/q carries margin for comprehension passages, which is the one place a real paper's per-question average misleads: a passage is read once and answered five times.

Against RRB Scale-II the same pace is a little tighter than its 45 s/q sections and far more generous than Financial Awareness and Computer Knowledge, which give 40 questions 15 minutes — 22.5 s/q. Nothing in Preppy's default drills at that speed, and a track that wanted to could say so in its pattern.

Marking

FR-3.3Set per section, so configurable per paper. Shipped default +1 correct, −0.25 wrong, 0 unattempted — a maximum of +55.00 and a minimum of −13.75 on the shipped pattern.

The shipped default marks Professional Knowledge like everything else, and the real exam does not

In both the SO preliminary and the SO main, Professional Knowledge carries two marks a question while the other tests carry one — 25 questions for 50 marks, 50 questions for 100. Since the penalty is a quarter of what the question is worth, a wrong PK answer costs 0.5 there and 0.25 everywhere else. A uniform +1/−0.25, as shipped, understates PK by half.

This is a choice about the default, not a limit: marks are stored per section, so a pattern can set PK to +2/−0.5 on the Paper pattern screen and the engine, the score band and the leaderboard all follow. It is left uniform because a nightly drill is read as "how many did I get right", and doubling one section makes the headline score incomparable with the four-section papers already sat.

  • Two decimals, displayed signed when negative.
  • A question left Marked for Review with no option selected is unattempted and scores 0.
  • Per-section marksCorrect and marksNegative overridable in the paper file.
  • No sectional cut-offs. Rank is total score alone.

Not reached vs skipped

FR-3.4Unattempted questions are recorded in two categories, both scoring 0.

CategoryDefinitionWhat it tells the student
SkippedOpened the question, did not answer, section endedA decision — they saw it and passed
Not reachedNever opened it before the section endedA pacing failure — they ran out of time

These map onto the live palette: Not answered (red) becomes Skipped; Not visited (grey) becomes Not reached. The remedies are opposite — skipping too much is a confidence problem, not reaching is a speed problem.

Stored per attempt: total_score · section_scores_json · attempted · correct · wrong · skipped · not_reached · time_spent_sec · fullscreen_exits · tab_switches. Accuracy, rank, average and cohort size are computed on read, never stored.

04The daily clock

One repeating 24-hour cycle. Almost every state is computed from the clock on read. Exactly one job does durable work.

—
Admin prepares

Upload, validate, dry-run and schedule a paper for any future date.

its unlock time
The paper opens

22:00 in this example, and set per paper. The dashboard flips from a countdown to the entry card.

its last entry
Entry closes

23:15 here. No attempt may start at or after it; the last possible start is one second before. An admin may move this while the paper runs (FR-6.9.8), and may end the paper outright before it (FR-6.9.7).

its hard stop
Everybody is finished

Midnight here. The last entrant's minutes expire, every figure on the paper stops moving, and the archive opens to the whole cohort — including whoever never sat it. Computed on read.

as each one hands in
The board moves

A result joins the leaderboard and the ranks the moment it is scored, so the board changes through the day. Streaks wait for the paper to close, or the four who have not sat it yet would watch a streak break and heal an hour later. Computed on read; anything still open is scored first.

13:00 & 01:00
Finalise

Force-submit and score any attempt still open. The only scheduled job, run twice a day.

FR-4.1Entry closes before the window does. The tail is one whole paper long, so a student starting at the last possible moment still gets every minute the paper allows. 23:15 and midnight are one example; each paper carries its own window, chosen as a day and a time for each end, and it may cross midnight at either — entry closing at 1 AM on the 29th, or the last attempt finishing at 1:45. A paper that opens on the 28th is the 28th's paper everywhere it is counted, whatever hours it occupies.

FR-4.2Handing the paper in settles it, for you. The result page, the answers, your rank, the comparison and the board all arrive the moment the attempt is scored. While entry is still open the cohort-derived figures are a standing among those who have finished so far, and every screen showing one says so far: a number that will move is never dressed up as final.

FR-4.3Correct answers and solutions open to you when you hand the paper in, and to everybody once entry closes — whether or not they sat it (FR-6.3.2). They are never shown to somebody who can still take the paper, which is the property that actually matters; the gate is the viewer's own attempt, not the clock.

What this reversal costs, stated plainly

These two used to wait for the paper's hard stop, so that nobody held an answer anybody else could still use. Waiting bought one thing — a student cannot pass the answers to a friend who has not sat it yet — and cost a great deal: a paper sat at nine in the morning gave its taker nothing until midnight, which is most of what a mock test is for.

The trade is deliberate. The cohort is small and known to each other, the leaderboard never resets, and collusion is a thing a person does, not a thing a schema prevents. What the product still refuses to do is make it easy: nothing is visible to anybody who has not finished the paper, so no one can read the answers and then decide whether to sit it.

If a paper ever matters enough to want the old behaviour back, the admin has the tool: end it for everyone (FR-6.9.7), which closes entry and settles every figure at once.

FR-5.1There is no public sign-up. Accounts exist only because an admin created them.

FR-5.2Admin attempts are always dry runs. The admin can take any paper — draft, scheduled or live — and it is never counted, never ranked, never on the leaderboard, never in any aggregate. This replaces v1.1's exclusion logic threaded through every query with a single flag set at attempt creation. The admin cannot compete, by construction.

FR-5.3Students see nothing about each other except the leaderboard. The leaderboard is the only surface exposing another person's data, and it exposes exactly its own columns. Everywhere else, a student sees only their own data.

Specifically removed to honour this:

  • Cohort average comparison bars on the result page.
  • "% of the cohort got this right" in the answer review.
  • Participation counts on archive rows.
  • Any indication of who has or has not submitted a paper still running. Given up with FR-4.2: a live board shows a student's paper count going up, which says they have sat it. Participation, never answers.
  • Any view of another student's answers, timings, attempt detail or violation counters.

06Functional requirements

6.1  Authentication

FR-6.1.1Login accepts username and password only.

Implementation. Supabase Auth is the identity store. It requires an email, so a username maps to a synthetic internal address — <username>@preppy.local — never shown, never collected, never sent mail. No SMTP is configured.

  • Usernames 3–20 chars, [a-z0-9_], lowercase-normalised, unique.
  • Hashing, sessions and refresh are Supabase Auth's job. No custom crypto.

FR-6.1.2Admin-created users must change their password on first login.

FR-6.1.3No self-service recovery — there is no email to send it to. The admin resets passwords, and the login screen says so.

FR-6.1.4Starting a test invalidates that user's other sessions.

FR-6.1.5Your account is yours. One screen holds both things anybody may change about themselves: the display name the board and the archive use, and the password. Renaming yourself needed an admin for no reason anyone could name — the username is the login and stays where it is, but what the board calls you is not an administrative fact. The action reads the signed-in user from the session and never from the form, so it cannot reach another account however it is submitted. While a first-login password change is being forced, the screen shows only that: your name is not the thing standing between you and the paper.

6.2  Home screen — logged out

Product name, the pattern summary, and a single Log in action. No sign-up link. Where the paper stands decides the rest: a live countdown to the next paper's unlock when one is scheduled, “open now” with its last entry while one is running, and the plain truth when there is neither — a fabricated countdown to a moment nothing happens at is worse than no countdown.

FR-6.2.1No public leaderboard preview. With five members, an "anonymised top 5" is the entire cohort.

FR-6.3.2Every closed test is fully browsable — all questions, options, keys and solutions — whether or not the student attempted it.

FR-6.3.3For an attempted test the review overlays the student's own answer, per-question time, and whether each unattempted question was skipped or not reached.

FR-6.3.4A missed test can be reviewed but never attempted.

Panel 3 — Leaderboard

The whole cohort, inline, however many that is. The current user's row is highlighted.

Panel 4 — Timetable

The topic plan the series is cut from, day by day, from the first paper to the exam: what Professional Knowledge covers and the Quant, Reasoning and English item beside it. It is the same table for everybody and read-only on both sides — a student opens it to know what is coming, an admin to know what to set. The only thing it knows about the reader is today, which it marks.

FR-6.3.5The timetable is a plan, not state. No row of it is linked to a paper, no paper is required to follow it, and nothing about a student's progress is written to it. A day the plan names and no paper was set for is a fact about the week, not an error the product should report.

FR-6.3.6It renders as a table on a laptop and as a stacked card per day on a phone. Six columns of prose behind a sideways scroll is a timetable nobody checks.

FR-6.3.7The same table is reachable from the admin console, from one source through one component. An admin setting tomorrow's paper needs the row the cohort is looking at, not a second copy that may disagree.

Kahoot's look, an exam's mechanics. The palette covers only the current section — there is no route back to a finished one. The five shape-coded colour cards are Kahoot's signature device and they earn their place: shape carries the distinction independently of colour, so the brand motif satisfies an accessibility requirement. At section end, grey cells become not reached and red cells become skipped.

FR-6.4.1Layout. One question at a time, a palette rail beside it, and a header carrying section name, timer, progress and the violation counter.

FR-6.4.2One-way section navigation. Within a section the student jumps freely via the palette. They may move forward at any time. They may never return to a previous section.

FR-6.4.3Next Section is explicit, with a confirmation dialog summarising answered / skipped / not-reached counts and a warning that the section cannot be re-entered. On the final section it reads End Test.

FR-6.4.4Palette states. Five states, colour- and shape-coded, with a permanent legend and an explicit mapping to skipped / not reached at section end.

FR-6.4.5Controls. Save & Next · Mark for Review & Next · Clear Response · Previous · palette jump within-section · Next Section.

FR-6.4.6Sectional timer. On expiry the section locks and the next opens automatically. Unused time never carries over.

FR-6.4.7Timer authority. The server owns the clock. The client renders a countdown for display only; every response write returns authoritative remaining time.

FR-6.4.8Persistence. Every response writes to the server immediately (300 ms debounce) and mirrors to localStorage. Refresh, crash or network loss resumes at the right question with the right remaining time. Disconnect time is not refunded.

FR-6.4.9Offline tolerance. Non-blocking banner; responses queue locally and flush on reconnect. The timer keeps running.

FR-6.4.10Direction blocks. Shared passages, DI tables and puzzles render above the question, pinned and independently scrollable, on every question in the group.

FR-6.4.11Keyboard. 1–5 select, Enter saves and advances, M marks, ←/→ navigate. Visible focus rings throughout.

6.5  Full-screen & integrity

Policy: warn and count. Never auto-submit. Store two integers.

FR-6.5.1Begin requests full-screen. If the browser denies it, the test does not start.

FR-6.5.2Honest constraint. Every browser guarantees Esc exits full-screen and no web application can override that. Preppy treats full-screen as deterrence, not prevention, and says so in the briefing.

FR-6.5.3On violation an opaque overlay covers the question: "Return to full screen to continue." The section timer keeps running — otherwise leaving full-screen becomes a pause button.

FR-6.5.4Two counters, no log table. The attempt row carries fullscreen_exits and tab_switches, each an integer incremented in place. There is no event table, no timestamps, no durations, no event list — and therefore no retention policy to implement. The counters are part of the attempt and live as long as it does.

FR-6.5.5The live count shows in the test header. The result page shows the final two numbers. The admin sees the same two numbers.

FR-6.5.6The test never auto-submits on a violation. Only timer expiry or the paper’s own hard stop ends a test the student did not end.

FR-6.5.7Best-effort deterrents. Selection, copy, cut, paste, right-click and print are suppressed; Ctrl/⌘+P, Ctrl/⌘+S and F12 intercepted. Friction, not security — not counted or stored.

FR-6.5.8Device policy. Taking a test requires a viewport ≥ 1024 px. Smaller viewports get a friendly block explaining the real exam is desktop-only too.

Trade-off, stated plainly

Counters tell you that something happened, never when or for how long. If you ever suspect someone, you will have a number and no context.

For five people who know each other, that is the right trade. The counter's job is deterrence, and a number on a result page deters exactly as well as a timestamped log.

6.6  Results

Everything here arrives together, the moment the attempt is scored. A result you cannot place against anything is half a result, and telling somebody who finished at nine in the morning to come back at midnight for the other half is the behaviour this replaced.

  • Headline: the total out of what the paper is worth, animated count-up, full Kahoot celebration. The marks come from the paper, not from a constant.
  • Rank: "2nd of 5," at once (FR-4.2), and "2nd of 3 so far" while the rest of the cohort is still sitting it. A percentile beside it once the cohort passes 30 (FR-6.7.10); below that the only percentiles available are 0, 20, 40, 60 and 80, and each moves twenty points when one person has an off day.
  • Sectional table: marks, attempted, right, wrong, unanswered, accuracy and time used, a row per section. A table rather than four cards, because eight figures a section is what a section is and columns let them be compared downwards as well as across.
  • Pacing verdict from the skipped/not-reached split: "You never reached 4 questions in Quant. That's a speed problem, not a knowledge problem."
  • Slowest three questions per section.
  • Integrity: the two counters.
  • Where this sits — at once: your score, the best on this paper, and what the room averaged, as three bars on one axis. Two figures, never a name, and only ever shown to somebody who has already finished the paper.
  • Leaderboard delta: cumulative rank before → after.
  • Review answers — enabled from the moment you hand in.

6.7  Leaderboard

FR-6.7.1Ranking is total score inside a window of recent papers — the last seven by default, the last thirty or all time on request. Dry runs excluded, which excludes admin attempts automatically. The window counts papers on the system, not papers you sat: somebody who has missed the last seven is not on the default board at all, which is the point of it.

FR-6.7.8The default is the last seven papers. A board that never resets slowly becomes a record of who joined first, and the earliest member is unreachable by the newest however well they are now doing. Recent form is the part a student can still do something about, so that is what they see first. All time is one press away and still never resets (FR-6.7.5 is unchanged: a new member starts from zero).

FR-6.7.2Tie-breaks, in order: higher total → higher average → higher accuracy % → higher best single paper. All four are measured inside the window on show, so “best” on the default board means the best of the last seven.

Average sits second on purpose. Within a window it is the one figure that separates the same total earned over fewer papers from the same total earned over more — and the one earned over fewer is the better performance. The table's columns are in this order, so reading it left to right is reading the rule.

Rows equal on all four genuinely share a place. Less time taken and an earlier first attempt still order them, so the table cannot shuffle between reloads, but neither is a ranking criterion any more.

FR-6.7.7A result joins the board when it is scored, not when its paper closes. A paper nobody has sat yet is not on the board at all; it joins at its close whether or not anybody sat it, because skipping a paper has to count against a streak. Streaks alone are measured over papers that have closed — on a live one, the first student to hand in would otherwise break everybody else's.

FR-6.7.3Computed on read. Five students across a few hundred tests is a trivial aggregate. A snapshot table would be more machinery than the query it replaces — and it would need its own invalidation story after a rescore.

FR-6.7.4Voided and dry-run attempts excluded. Negative cumulative totals are possible and displayed honestly.

FR-6.7.5A new member starts from zero. A sixth student joining in month two begins at 0 cumulative points and climbs from there. Accepted deliberately; the Last 30 filter is the view that stays meaningful for them.

Filters: Last 7 papers (default) · Last 30 papers · All time · a single paper's rank list. Counted in papers rather than days, since a day may hold more than one. The dashboard's inline panel uses the same default, so the two never disagree about who is first. Top 3 render as an animated podium: three lit panels, each a step lower than the one before it, first in the middle, with the place worn as a filled medal — gold, silver, bronze. The medals are fixed in both themes; one that changed colour with the theme would stop being a medal. Solid metal fills were tried and abandoned: three flat rectangles where there had been one card with light in it.

FR-6.7.9The Total column says what it is out of. Its header reads “Total of 165”: the sum of the perfect scores of the papers in the window on show. A cumulative number with no ceiling beside it is a number nobody can place — 20 is excellent over one paper and poor over seven.

FR-6.7.10A percentile, once the board is bigger than 30. The share of the board scoring strictly below you, which is the definition the exam itself uses — so the top of the board is never 100, and a tie shares a percentile as it shares a rank. Below the threshold the column is absent, not empty: with five students the only percentiles available are 0, 20, 40, 60 and 80, each moving twenty points when one person has an off day, which is a coarser measure than “2nd of 5” wearing a decimal point.

FR-6.7.6The admin sees the same board, in the console. Same figures, same filters, no row of their own — their attempts are dry runs and are counted nowhere. Reaching it used to mean leaving the console for the student view, which is an odd trip to make to answer “who is actually turning up”; the per-paper rank list is the view an admin wants most, since it is the night-by-night one the cumulative board hides.

6.8  Streaks P1

Current streak, longest streak, personal-best celebration, podium. No badge system — streaks and the podium carry the motivational load.

FR-6.8.1A streak counts papers, not days. Sit the paper and the run goes up; skip one that ran and it breaks, including the morning paper on a day you sat the evening one. It counted days for a while, so that somebody who always sat the evening paper was not punished for skipping the morning one — but that made a two-paper day worth the same as a one-paper day, and a student who sat both watched their streak stand still. A paper is the unit this product is about.

FR-6.8.2Which papers count is per student, like everything else since FR-4.2: every paper that has closed, plus one still open that you have already sat. A paper still open that you have not sat is not in your run at all — counting it would break everybody's streak the moment the first student handed in, and heal it again an hour later. The run spans every paper ever, not the window on show, or inside “Last 7” a streak would be capped at seven.

6.9  Admin console

At /admin, gated on role = admin server-side.

Paper ingestion

  1. Upload the paper as a .json file, optionally with images.
  2. Parse strictly. Invalid JSON is reported with its line and column.
  3. Validate — blocking errors separated from warnings, each with the path to the field.
  4. Preview — every question rendered in the exact student interface, with key and solution. Cannot be skipped.
  5. Dry run (optional) — take the paper end to end in the real test engine.
  6. Schedule — assign a date; save as draft or publish.

FR-6.9.12A paper can be named on the way in, and renamed at any point after. The file's own title is the default, because most papers number themselves and need nothing else; the upload screen's name box overrides it, and Manage changes it later. Applied after validation, so a typed name cannot make an invalid file look valid. Nothing keys off the name — it is a label, and two papers called the same thing on one day is a real thing that happens.

FR-6.9.1Publishing is never automatic. A paper reaches SCHEDULED only by explicit admin action after preview.

Blocking errorsWarnings — still publishable

Dry run

FR-6.9.2The admin can take any test — DRAFT, SCHEDULED, LIVE or CLOSED — as a dry run, using the real test engine: real sectional timers, real full-screen, real scoring. The attempt is stored with is_dry_run = true, excluded from the leaderboard, ranks and every aggregate.

FR-6.9.2aA dry run is a rehearsal, not a record. Starting one removes the admin's previous dry run on that paper, and the Attempts screen never lists them at all. The row exists while the rehearsal runs, because the engine needs somewhere to put the answers — but an admin who walks a paper three times should leave one row behind, not three, and the console should never imply more people sat a paper than did.

This is how a paper gets proofread at full speed, and it is why FR-5.2 can be so blunt: the admin has a first-class way to take tests that is structurally incapable of competing.

Users, tests & export

Create a single user; bulk-create from CSV; auto-generate passwords; rename anyone — students and admins alike — by pressing the name; reset a password; activate/deactivate; view any student's attempt history. Browse every test with all questions, keys and solutions, and edit any of them after publication.

FR-6.9.6A reset asks twice. Setting a new password ends every session the account has open, including a student halfway through a paper, so it is two presses rather than one. The username is the login and never changes; the display name is what the board and the archive show, so it is editable at any time — by an admin here, and by the person themselves (FR-6.1.5).

FR-6.9.3Rescore. Editing a key on a closed test offers Rescore, which recomputes every affected attempt. Because the leaderboard is computed on read, nothing else needs recomputing — the board is correct on the next page load. Affected students see a "this test was rescored" notice.

FR-6.9.4One item statistic. The admin sees % correct per question and nothing else per item. Any item below 10% correct is flagged for key review. This is the minimum needed to catch a bad key, which is the only reason item statistics exist here.

FR-6.9.5A single Export question bank (JSON) action producing every test ever published, with sections, questions, options, keys and solutions.

Managing a paper that is already out

Everything above is about getting a paper right before it runs. This is the half the first draft of this document did not have, and its absence was felt the first time something went wrong at ten past ten: a scheduled paper was furniture. Its window was fixed at the moment it was scheduled, it ended when arithmetic said it ended, and a mistake in it could only be corrected one key at a time. Manage is the screen for the other case.

The rule running through all five controls: each is scoped to what is still safe, and each says what it does to the people sitting the paper, in figures. A paper with twelve minutes left and two students in it is not a row in a table and the screen should not pretend it is.

FR-6.9.7End it now, for everyone. Whatever the window says. Nobody new can start, every attempt still running is submitted and scored exactly where it stands, and answers, the archive and the leaderboard open from that instant. Two presses, the second naming how many students are in the room. It cannot be undone — a student cut off at question thirty has been cut off — which is why it asks.

FR-6.9.8Move the window while it runs. The flexible half: somebody has not sat it yet and you want to hold the door open a little longer. Moving the last moment to start moves the finish with it, so whoever starts at the new time still gets the whole paper — the FR-4.1 guarantee is not negotiable, it is arithmetic. Once the paper has opened its opening time is fixed, because moving that would rewrite when attempts already running were allowed to begin.

FR-6.9.9Change what the questions are worth. Marking per section, for one paper, after which every finished attempt on it is scored again. Same reasoning as FR-6.9.3: the board is computed on read, so making the stored scores agree with the marking is the whole operation.

FR-6.9.10Replace the questions. When the file itself was wrong rather than one key in it, upload a corrected version over the paper. It keeps its id, its night and its place in the schedule, so every link still works and nobody schedules it again; whoever has not sat it yet simply gets the new version. Refused once anybody has sat it — their answers point at the questions this would delete, and a score against questions that no longer exist is a number with nothing behind it.

FR-6.9.11Delete it. A paper nobody has sat goes whether or not it has opened. Being unable to remove a live one was the real gap: it left a broken paper on screen until midnight. One that has been sat needs an explicit confirmation that states how many attempts go with it, because they go with it — answers, scores, leaderboard entries and all.

Why this is not just “unschedule”

Unscheduling was already there and does none of this. It is refused the moment a paper opens, which is precisely when you need it, and it hides the paper rather than stopping the clock — a student mid-attempt would carry on. The five controls above exist because hiding and ending are different things, and only one of them was ever built.

6.10  Exam tracks P3

Three things this document parked in phase 3 are one mechanism, and building them separately would have produced three half-answers. Computer Awareness and General Awareness as toggleable sections wants sections that are not always there. Disciplines beyond CSE wants the Professional Knowledge section to be about something else. A second exam track with its own pattern wants a different paper shape entirely. One thing answers all three: a track.

FR-6.10.1A track is an exam somebody is preparing for. It owns an ordered pattern of sections and a set of papers. A student follows exactly one, and sees only its papers, its archive and its board.

FR-6.10.2A track decides which sections it has, in what order. Any of the six codes, any number of them. The four-section pattern stops being the product's and becomes one track's.

FR-6.10.3A track names its own sections. Professional Knowledge is (CSE) on one track and (Agriculture) on another, and neither needs a section code of its own. A name is a label: correcting it corrects it everywhere the section has ever appeared.

FR-6.10.4A paper is checked against the pattern of the exam it is for. A file whose sections are not that exam's, in that order, is refused — and told which sections that exam does have, rather than “unknown section”.

FR-6.10.5Boards do not mix. One leaderboard per track. A table ranking people against papers they were never offered is not a ranking.

FR-6.10.9A student may look at another exam's board. Not mixed into theirs — FR-6.10.5 stands — but chosen, one exam at a time, and labelled as somebody else's. A board is names and scores; it gives away no question, no key and no solution, and those stay locked to the reader's own exam (FR-6.10.1). The exam that is theirs is marked as theirs, so the others read as visiting.

FR-6.10.6Two tracks may run a paper at the same moment on the same day. A student follows one of them, so there is no choice being forced and nothing to prevent. Within one track, overlapping windows are still refused.

FR-6.10.7A track can be closed, not deleted. Its papers, attempts and board stay; no new paper can be scheduled on it. The last open track cannot be closed. This is the shape deactivating an account already has: the login stops, the history stays.

FR-6.10.8With one track, nothing on screen mentions one. No switcher, no column, no picker. A choice between one thing is not a choice, and an install preparing for one exam must never be asked to think about this.

The constant that had to go

SECTION_CODES was the four codes of one exam, in sequence, and the whole application read the shape of a paper off it — the validator's ordering, the pattern form, the blank template, the question bands. It is ALL_SECTION_CODES now and answers one question only: is this a real code. Everything that wants the order asks a pattern. That rename is most of what this feature was.

6.11  Practice P3

A paper in the archive could be read and not re-sat, which is the wrong way round for the thing it is for. Reading the solution tells you the answer; sitting it again under the clock tells you whether you would have found it.

FR-6.11.1A student may re-sit any paper already open to them, as practice. The engine, the timers and the marking are the real ones.

FR-6.11.2Practice is counted nowhere. No rank, no board, no movement in the counted score. It is the dry run the admin already had, asked for on purpose.

FR-6.11.3Practice is offered on a paper already open to you and on no other — you have finished it, or it has closed. The same gate the archive opens on, so the two cannot drift. Practising a paper you can still sit for real would be sitting it twice, the second time knowing the questions, and it is the counted attempt that would be worth less for it.

FR-6.11.4One practice result per paper. Practising again replaces the one before it rather than piling up.

6.12  Installable, and readable offline P3

FR-6.12.1Preppy is installable: a manifest, icons and a standalone display, so a paper opens from a home screen rather than from a tab among thirty.

FR-6.12.2Past papers already opened are readable without a connection, with their images.

FR-6.12.3A paper being sat is never served from a cache, at any stage. Its clock belongs to the server, and a timer read from a cache is the wrong timer.

FR-6.12.4Nothing else is kept. Not the dashboard, not the board, not the console, not an account page. Each of them moves through the day, and an answer that is out of date by the time it is read is worse than no answer.

FR-6.12.5Signing out forgets what was kept. A cache belongs to the browser rather than to the account, and students share laptops.

Why caching the archive gives nothing away

This looked at first like a trade against section 9: the answer key never reaches the browser, and offline reading means writing papers to a device. It is not a trade. The archive only ever renders a paper the reader has finished, or one that has closed — and for those the key is already in the HTML the server sent them. Keeping a copy leaks nothing they cannot already read. A paper that has not opened is never rendered to them at all, so it cannot enter the cache: only a 200 is stored, and a locked paper does not return one.

07The paper format

One paper is one JSON file. The format is rigid and the parser strict — it fails loudly rather than guessing. The full spec is section 7 of the engineering reference; format/schema.json mirrors the rules for editor validation.

  • JSON only, parsed as written. Curly quotes, trailing commas and comments are errors, not silently repaired. Papers are written in a code editor, not a word processor.
  • Question numbers run straight through the paper from 1, and each section holds exactly its own band. How many there are is the track's pattern, not a constant.
  • Everything except number, text, options and answer is optional on a question.

FR-7.1directions is mandatory infrastructure. Reading comprehension, cloze tests, DI sets and seating-arrangement puzzles are a large share of a real IBPS paper and are unrepresentable without it. DI tables are structured headers and rows, never space-aligned text.

FR-7.2A fill-in template.json, a filled sample.json and schema.json ship in format/, and npm run check validates a paper exactly as the upload does. Format drift is the most likely cause of a missed night.

FR-7.3Every question carries exactly four options, keyed A to D. Three is refused and so is five — an error, not a warning. The number is stated once, in OPTION_LABELS, and the validator, the JSON schema, the blank template, the key editor and the engine's option palette all read it from there.

The real exam prints five, and this is the one place Preppy knowingly differs

IBPS says so plainly in its Information Handout: "Out of the five answers to a question only one will be the correct answer." Four is a decision about this product, not a reading of the exam — the cohort writing the questions found the fifth distractor was usually filler, and a shorter option card reads faster under a clock.

What it costs, stated so nobody has to work it out later: a blind guess is worth 25% here against 20% on the real paper. Against −0.25 marking that makes guessing very slightly profitable on Preppy and clearly unprofitable on the real thing, so a student who guesses freely here will find the real paper punishes it harder. Read a Preppy score as a shade generous on that one axis.

Migration 0008 puts the rule in the table as well, since save_paper, a key correction and a hand-run UPDATE do not pass through the validator.

One job; everything else computed on read

FR-10.1Paper status is derived from the clock on every read: a paper reads LIVE inside its own window and CLOSED after it, with one stored exception (ended_at, FR-6.9.7). Unlock, entry-close and the cohort-wide archive unlock need no job and therefore cannot fail. What a student may see is not a clock question at all any more — it is whether they have finished the paper (FR-4.3).

FR-10.2The leaderboard, ranks, accuracy, averages and streaks are computed on read from the attempts table. With five students this is a few dozen rows. Nothing is precomputed, so nothing can go stale — and a rescore needs no cache invalidation.

Writes that must land together — saving a paper, starting an attempt, a key correction with its rescore — are each one Postgres function in supabase/migrations/, so each is all or nothing. Those functions are callable with the secret key only.

CronTimeWork
Finalise13:00, 01:00Force-submit and score any attempt still in progress. That is the whole job, and the admin can also run it on demand.

FR-10.3The job is idempotent — re-running produces identical output — and can be triggered manually from the admin console.

The v1.1 publish-reminder job is dropped. With no SMTP configured there is nowhere to send a reminder, so it would have needed a whole notification channel to be useful. Instead the admin dashboard leads with today's papers and where each one stands. For the reminder itself, a recurring 21:45 phone alarm does the job with no code.

The one security rule

FR-10.4Never query Supabase from the browser. All database access happens in server code using the secret key — sb_secret_..., or the legacy service_role key it replaces — held in a server-only environment variable. Row Level Security is enabled on every table with deny-all policies as a backstop, so the publishable key the browser does hold reads nothing.

Deliberately simpler than per-table RLS policies. With six accounts, writing and debugging fine-grained policies is more risk than it removes — and one memorable rule is easier to follow correctly than a dozen policies.

13Security & privacy

FR-13.1Correct answers and solutions are never sent to the client during a live test, in any payload, including for questions already answered. The test API returns questions and options only.

  • Password hashing and sessions are Supabase Auth's responsibility. No custom crypto.
  • All database access is server-side with the secret key; RLS deny-all as a backstop.
  • Admin routes gated by role server-side, not by route obscurity.
  • Rate limits on login, response writes and paper upload. Uploads restricted by MIME type and size.
  • Minimal PII — a username, a display name, and a synthetic internal address that receives no mail.
  • Minimal behavioural data — two integers per attempt. No event log, no timestamps, nothing to retain. Students are told in the briefing what the two counters are and that the admin can see them. One exception, and it is not Preppy's own store: Vercel's Speed Insights and Web Analytics record timestamped page-load and page-view measurements. Ids are stripped from the URL before either sends it, but a city and a 24-hour visitor hash remain. Section 9 of the engineering reference lists every field and is honest about what six people does to the word anonymous.
  • FR-5.3 makes the privacy posture structural: a student's data is visible to that student, to the admin, and — for the leaderboard columns only — to the cohort.

Things the build added that this document did not ask for

Each of these came out of the work rather than the plan, and each is load-bearing enough to belong in the spec now.

MechanismWhy it exists
Optimistic locking on the answer key
key_version on tests and attempts		An answer key corrected while somebody is mid-paper would otherwise let their submission bank against the old key. A submission carrying a stale version is detected and rescored instead
Rate limits counted in the database
a table of their own plus three functions		Counting in server memory does not work on a serverless host: every instance keeps its own tally, so the limit is however many instances are running
Explicit session revocation
revoke_user_sessions		FR-6.1.4 asked for one attempt in one place. Reaching into the auth schema to end the other sessions needs elevated rights, so it is a function rather than a query
Paged reads
selectAll		A Supabase project caps an API response at 1,000 rows by default — its setting, not PostgREST's, whose own default is no limit — and a capped response looks exactly like a complete one. The leaderboard would have started losing attempts silently as the history grew
Private image storage
plus an app route that checks permission		A diagram for a DI set is part of the question. Served straight from Storage it would leak the paper before it opened
The schema tested as SQL
tests/schema.test.ts on in-process Postgres		The functions that must be atomic are the ones a type system cannot check. They are now exercised as SQL rather than trusted
A window per paper
on tests, with the global setting demoted to a default		The times were a constant, then a global setting, and both made a second paper in a day impossible. Each paper now carries its own window, chosen when it is scheduled. The hard stop stays derived as entry close plus one paper, so the full-attempt guarantee holds whatever is chosen — and since 0010 and 0011 both ends of the window are chosen as a day and a time, so it may cross midnight at either, because a paper belongs to its date rather than to the hours it occupies
More than one paper a day
and one attempt at a time		A date no longer identifies a paper. Scheduling refuses windows that overlap, and a database index allows a student only one counted attempt in progress across every paper, since two at once would split 45 minutes across two clocks
A paper settles per student, not per clock
the gate is your own finished attempt		Everything cohort-derived used to wait for the paper's hard stop. A paper sat at nine in the morning then gave its taker nothing until midnight, which is most of what a mock test is for. Now handing in settles it for you; what is still refused is showing anything to somebody who can still sit the paper (FR-4.2, FR-4.3)
A paper can be managed after it has gone out
tests.ended_at, plus three functions		A scheduled paper used to be furniture: fixed window, arithmetic ending, corrections one key at a time. That is fine until something goes wrong at ten past ten. An admin can now end it for everybody, move its window, change its marking, replace its questions, or delete it — each scoped to what is still safe (FR-6.9.7–11)
An early end that overrules the clock
ended_at beats every derived time, and can only bring the stop forward		The one exception to “everything is computed on read”, and a deliberate one. Taking the earlier of the stamp and the derived stop means a stale row can shorten a paper but never push one past the day its constraint keeps it inside
A dry run that does not accumulate
Each start removes the last; the console never lists them		They were counted nowhere but shown everywhere, so the Attempts screen implied more people had sat a paper than had
The reads tested against the real project
npm run test:live, read-only, opt-in		A select that forgets a column is invisible to TypeScript and to an in-process Postgres. It had already shipped twice — once leaving Export question bank a 500 that nothing noticed, because nothing had ever called it
Nothing keys off the calendar dayAnswers, the leaderboard and the archive all waited for midnight or 00:01. They waited for the paper's own hard stop next, and now for the student's own hand-in — each step moving the unlock closer to the person it is for
Numbered migrations
supabase/migrations/		One re-runnable schema file worked while every change was additive. A numbered chain with a runner records what has been applied, so dev and production cannot silently diverge Collapsed to two files — the baseline and everything since — once it was clear the intermediate steps had never been applied to any database. The next change is a third file.
A design system
app/globals.css		Seven flat hues and three greys became raw scales plus a semantic layer (--surface, --text, --accent) that the components address instead of colours. Dark mode follows from that rather than being bolted on. It was built twice: the first went by Material’s advice for a neutral product, which tinted the brand out of every surface and left a room of brown-greys that read as switched off rather than dark. The landing page’s violet is the app’s now. Gold joins violet, because rank, streaks and the podium had no colour of their own. Three faces replace one: Nunito displays, IBM Plex Sans is what the questions are actually read in, Plex Mono holds figures that must not shift. Kahoot’s option shapes stay, and are now the wordmark too
Two builds
build:local and build:prod		Production gates on typecheck and the full suite and ships no source maps; local keeps them so a stack trace is worth reading

Phase 4, if it happens

Nothing is planned. What would earn a place: a second cohort large enough to want per-section percentiles · question-level analytics across papers, once there is a year of them · a way to hand a track to a second admin.

What is still unproven

The schema and its functions are exercised against real Postgres, 444 tests cover the pure logic, and npm run test:live now runs every read the app makes against the real project — a suite that exists because a select which forgets a column is invisible to both TypeScript and an in-process Postgres, and had already shipped twice. What no automated test reaches is the half that writes: signing in, starting and submitting an attempt, and reading an image out of Storage. The engineering reference has a ten-minute walkthrough that covers those.
